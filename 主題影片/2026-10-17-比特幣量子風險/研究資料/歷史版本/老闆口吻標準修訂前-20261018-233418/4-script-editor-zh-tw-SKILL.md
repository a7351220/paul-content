# Script Editor ZH-TW

Read [voice-zh-TW.md](../market-script-writing/references/voice-zh-TW.md) completely before editing.

Edit the completed narration-only script. Do not research, add claims, change sourced numbers, replace uncertainty with confidence, rearrange approved slide jobs, or insert visual directions and citation inventories into the spoken artifact. Preserve user-approved wording, terminology, and rhythm unless they conflict with evidence.

Require `house_voice_profile: market-house-voice-zh-tw-v1` for both weekly and topic scripts. Treat mode as a structure and pacing choice, not permission to use a separate presenter voice. Apply a rejected weekly phrase or topic habit to the shared correction history and scan both modes for the same pattern in future work.

If the draft lacks evidence needed for a clean sentence, return a specific gap marker. Do not fill the gap with generic language.

## Editing Workflow

### 1. Recover the Contract

Record audience and assumed knowledge, learning outcome, approved story order and slide jobs, user-approved phrases and term forms, rejected patterns, research cutoff, and audit conditions.

Apply a user correction as a whole-script rule. Search for every sentence with the same underlying habit, not only the quoted wording.

### 2. Protect the Evidence

For each factual or analytical sentence, preserve its type: actual, forecast, management claim, analyst estimate, interpretation, scenario, or uncertainty.

Flag any fixed observation period, threshold, confirmation rule, multi-signal formula, or top/reversal condition that lacks an explicit method source. Remove the formula and state only the supported observation, or return:

```text
[需要補明確的方法來源；目前只能描述這個數字代表什麼，不能寫成固定判斷公式。]
```

Do not turn a partial product price into a conclusion about the entire market. Do not mix spot, contract, company-average, and analyst-assumption figures.

### 3. Edit by Page Role

#### Opening page

- Build one visible current situation.
- Name the tension the viewer is trying to judge.
- State the lesson before stacking company results or exact figures.
- Keep the first two or three sentences free of unnecessary acronyms and caveats.
- Use at most one essential exact number.
- Keep the delivery calm and patient without filler or manufactured excitement.

Reject an opening that could introduce any unrelated market topic after replacing the company names.

#### Definition page

- Introduce a term no earlier than needed and no later than first use.
- Use the shortest plain-language identity that unlocks the next claim.
- Explain products by application, buyer, demand source, supply pressure, and investor relevance before technical properties.
- Delete a definition that does not help the listener understand a later number or conclusion.

#### Data page

- Name the object, price or claim type, period, change, and implication.
- Put the number next to the comparison or reason that gives it meaning.
- Keep actuals, forecasts, and analyst assumptions visibly distinct.
- Prefer real expectation gaps or market reactions for claims about stock behavior.
- Treat changes inside one analyst model as model mechanics, not proof of market behavior.
- Move secondary methodology and source scope to production notes unless they prevent an overclaim.

#### Ending page

- Answer the opening question directly.
- Give one reusable distinction, decision, or watch item.
- Do not introduce a new source, date, company, or figure.
- Remove repeated summaries, report-style qualifications, slogans, and generic calls to action.
- Make the final sentence name the concrete mistake, distinction, or unresolved variable that matters.

### 4. Control Source Attribution

Keep complete citations in production notes. Name a source in narration only when its identity matters to the claim, including forecasts, institutional frameworks, management statements, disputes, or conflicting estimates.

At first spoken mention, identify the source only as far as necessary. Do not read its corporate biography or repeat the source name before each sentence from the same document.

### 5. Run the First-Use Audit

Build a ledger of every acronym, institution, product class, price type, and specialist term. Verify its first spoken occurrence, shortest necessary explanation, whether the listener needs it, and whether the approved short form remains consistent.

If a term is explained after first use, move or rewrite the introduction. If the explanation is longer than its role in the story, shorten it or move it to notes.

### 6. Run the Global Pattern Sweep

Search the whole script for filler openings, fake questions, repeated transitions, decorative modifiers, vague conclusions, source-name repetition, report-language scaffolding, disconnected technical definitions, numerical restatements, unsupported method language, and repeated endings.

When one occurrence is rejected, fix every occurrence of the pattern. Preserve legitimate uses that serve a different function.

### Page-level expansion check

If expansion was requested, compare every page with the saved pre-edit baseline. Enforce the user's multiplier on each page, not just the total, while retaining locked page count and useful conclusions. Check added passages for new facts, clearly marked hypothetical situations, concrete actions or distinct consequences. Remove semantic repetition even when wording differs; replacing “needs time” with “prepare early” does not satisfy expansion. Keep the count table outside the spoken script. Do not exempt approved opening text when the user explicitly asks to expand every page; retain its wording and add relevant material around it.

### 7. Run the Beginner Cold Read

Read in slide order without using knowledge from later slides. At each page, verify that a beginner can answer:

- What is being discussed?
- Where is the product used, or what role does the institution play?
- What kind of number is this?
- What changed, compared with what?
- Why does it matter to the investor question?
- What is known, estimated, interpreted, or still unknown?

If an answer relies on a later explanation, repair the logical jump. Do not solve confusion by adding an unrelated technical lecture.

## Acceptance Gates

Return a revised script only when all eight gates pass:

1. **Voice**: direct, relaxed, patient, and informative; no report voice, host banter, or deliberate familiarity.
2. **Opening**: a concrete situation, clear tension, and specific learning promise appear before the first dense data beat.
3. **First use**: every necessary acronym, institution, product, and price type is introduced before or at first use with the shortest useful identity.
4. **Teaching relevance**: definitions explain application and investor relevance; technical detail appears only when it changes the conclusion.
5. **Data meaning**: each core number carries a type, period or comparison base, direction, and implication; model output is not mistaken for market evidence.
6. **Source flow**: full citations stay in notes; spoken source names appear only when identity changes interpretation and are not repeated mechanically.
7. **Evidence fidelity**: no unsupported formula, fixed threshold, mixed price type, broadened product conclusion, or erased uncertainty remains.
8. **Ending and global consistency**: the ending answers the opening and gives one reusable distinction; the whole script is free of repeated rejected patterns and passes the beginner cold read.

Revise again if any gate fails. When a gate cannot pass without new research or a changed outline, return the exact gap and do not invent replacement prose.

## Output

Return the revised narration in its existing slide structure. Add a concise change note only when the user asks for one. Keep evidence gaps inline at the sentence or slide that cannot be repaired safely. If editing reveals that a slide must be inserted, deleted, reordered, or renamed, stop and return the structural change to the slide-map owner instead of silently changing headings.
